Privacy Policy

Pocket Fertilizer · last updated 3 August 2026

Pocket Fertilizer does not collect, transmit or store any personal information. There is no account to create, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no server holding your garden. Everything you record stays encrypted on the device you recorded it on.

What the app stores, and where

Your plots, beds, plantings, seed packets, journal entries, harvests, diagnoses and photos are written to storage on your own phone (IndexedDB, falling back to local storage) and are encrypted before they are written, using AES-256-GCM through the operating system's own cryptography.

If you set a passphrase in Settings, the key that unlocks that data is itself wrapped with a key derived from your passphrase by PBKDF2-SHA256 at 310,000 iterations. The passphrase is never stored and never transmitted. There is no recovery path — nobody, including the developer, can read or restore your data without it.

None of this data is sent anywhere. There is no backend service. Uninstalling the app removes it; there is no copy elsewhere to delete.

Network requests the app makes

The app works fully offline. When it does have a connection, it makes read-only requests to the following services. Each one is listed with exactly what leaves the device.

ServiceWhat is sentWhy
phzmapi.org A US ZIP code you typed in Look up the USDA hardiness zone
Open-Meteo Approximate latitude and longitude, or a place name you typed Frost dates from ten years of local records, current weather and the forecast
Wikipedia REST API A plant name Reference text on the library screen
cdnjs Nothing beyond the request itself Fallback only. The SQLite engine ships inside the app; this is used only by the web version if the bundled copy is unavailable.

No identifier, device fingerprint, advertising ID or account is attached to any of these requests. The app sends Referrer-Policy: no-referrer. Each of these services will see your IP address, as any website you visit does; their own privacy policies govern that.

Location

You can type a ZIP code or place name instead of sharing location. If you tap the option to use your device location, the coordinates are used once to fetch frost dates and weather, and are stored only in the encrypted vault on your device. They are not sent anywhere except to Open-Meteo as part of the weather request described above.

Camera, photos and microphone

  • Camera and photo library — used only when you choose to photograph a plant for the Plant Doctor or a seed packet for the seed bank. Photos are reduced in size and saved into the encrypted vault on the device. They are not uploaded, unless you have connected an AI assistant and explicitly ask for a photo to be analysed (see below).
  • Microphone — used only while you are holding the mic button to speak to the assistant. Speech is converted to text by your device's own speech recognition. Nothing is recorded or kept.

The optional AI assistant

The assistant is off until you add your own API key from Google (Gemini) or Anthropic (Claude). It is entirely optional and every other feature works without it.

If you turn it on, the questions you ask, the relevant parts of your garden records needed to answer them, and any photo you explicitly submit for analysis are sent directly from your device to the provider whose key you supplied. They do not pass through any server belonging to this app. That provider's own terms and privacy policy then apply to that data:

  • Google Gemini API terms
  • Anthropic privacy policy

Your API key is stored inside the encrypted vault on the device and is sent only to that provider. You can remove it at any time in Settings, which deletes it from the device.

Children

Pocket Fertilizer is a gardening tool for a general audience. It is not directed at children, collects nothing from anyone, and therefore collects nothing from children.

Data you export

Settings can export your garden as a .sqlite database or a .json backup. Those exports are written wherever you choose to save or send them and are not encrypted, so that other software can read them. Once a file leaves the app it is yours to look after.
