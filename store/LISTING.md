### Full description — 3,140 / 4,000

```
A gardener's pocket assistant. Plan beds on a grid, keep a real seed bank, get
a calendar built from your own frost dates, work out what is wrong with a sick
plant, and read the weather for what it actually means to your plants.

It works with no signal, because that is where gardens are. There is no
account, no cloud and no server. Everything you record is encrypted and stays
on your phone.


PLAN THE BEDS
Plots hold beds; beds are grids from 1x1 to 24x24 at any square size, 12 inches
by default — the classic square-foot method. Tap a square to plant it, or arm a
crop and paint across several.

Plantings stretch to any shape. A single cucumber that ate six square feet is
recorded as one sprawling plant, and the app remembers that footprint next
time. An area packed at proper spacing fills itself from real per-square-foot
figures: 1 tomato, 4 lettuce, 9 beets, 16 carrots. Either way it works out how
much seed to sow, adjusted for the germination rate of the actual packet you
linked, so an old packet at 40% asks for more than a fresh one.

Companion conflicts are flagged on the grid as you plant, measured edge to
edge, and rotation history tracks which plant family last held each bed.


TRACK THE SEED
Photograph a packet and it reads itself — crop, variety, brand, packed year,
germination rate. Or type it in. Each packet gets a viability level and an
expected germination percentage from that crop's seed life, so fading packets
surface before they waste bed space. Includes the ten-seed paper-towel test.


A CALENDAR THAT BUILDS ITSELF
Every packet in the bank generates sow, transplant and fall-sowing dates
anchored to your own frost dates — taken from ten years of actual daily lows at
your coordinates, not a zone average. Plus projected first-harvest dates and
warnings as seed reaches its viability limit.


DAYS TO MATURITY THAT LEARNS YOUR GARDEN
Published days-to-maturity is a single number pretending to be precise; it
swings thirty days between varieties of the same crop. So the app shows a range
and says where it came from. Record a first harvest and it starts learning.
After three, it plans with your number instead of the catalogue's, and the
season recap shows you that your garden runs, say, nine days slower than the
packet claims.


THE PLANT DOCTOR
Photograph a sick leaf. The image is analysed on the device for green, yellow
and brown ratios and whether damage concentrates at the margins, which
pre-selects the matching symptoms. 63 observations then run against 43
conditions — nutrient deficiencies, watering and light problems, fungal and
bacterial disease, pests, herbicide drift, pH lockout. You get ranked diagnoses
with confidence, a 48-hour action plan, prevention, and an honest note when the
top two are too close to call.


WEATHER, READ FOR PLANTS
Not just a forecast. Frost nights, heat above the 90F threshold where tomato
and pepper pollen goes sterile, wet spells that spread blight, dry weeks that
need deep watering — and a per-bed watering call that subtracts actual rainfall
from what each bed needs.


ASK IT
Type, or hold the mic and talk. The assistant is wired to the app's real
functions, so it acts rather than advises: "add tomatoes to plot A" opens the
bed with tomatoes ready to place, and warns you if it is short on sun or
already holds a bad neighbour. It also creates beds, logs harvests and
watering, and answers questions about your own records. Optional, and it needs
a free API key from Google; every other feature works without it.


JOURNAL AND SEASON RECAP
Log watering, feeding, amendments, treatments, weeding, soil tests and
harvests. The recap computes yield by crop, pounds per square foot by bed, cost
per pound, spend breakdown and recurring problems. Exports to CSV, to a real
SQLite database, or to a plain JSON backup. No lock-in.


SOURCED, AND HONEST ABOUT IT
Every figure for germination temperature, seed viability, watering and pH is
reconciled against a primary extension-service reference, and the app links to
it. Where the evidence is genuinely mixed — milk sprays on powdery mildew,
marigolds against nematodes, Japanese beetle traps — it says so instead of
picking a side. The seed packet always beats the app, and it tells you that too.


PRIVACY
No account. No analytics. No advertising. Nothing is uploaded. Your garden is
sealed with AES-256-GCM on the device, and a passphrase wraps that key with
310,000 rounds of PBKDF2 if you want one.
```

## App Review notes

Paste into **App Store Connect → App Review Information → Notes**. This is the
single field most likely to decide whether the first submission is approved,
because two reviewer questions are otherwise guaranteed: what the app does
beyond a website, and why a feature cannot be tested.

```
Pocket Fertilizer is a gardening planner. No account or login is required —
open it, enter a US ZIP code (for example 48103) or a place name, and every
feature unlocks.

TO SEE A FULL GARDEN IMMEDIATELY
Bottom bar -> Home -> menu (top right) -> Library -> "Load the demo garden".
This creates two beds, twelve plantings, six seed packets and harvest history
in one tap, so the planner, calendar and season recap all have data in them.

THE APP RUNS ENTIRELY ON THE DEVICE
The whole application is bundled inside the binary. It is not a web view
pointed at a website, and it is fully functional in airplane mode: the 60-crop
database, the planner, the seed bank, the calendar, the Plant Doctor's 43
conditions and the encrypted SQLite store are all local. Only weather, the
hardiness-zone lookup and the optional assistant use the network, and each one
degrades to a bundled offline default.

NATIVE INTEGRATION
Camera and photo library for diagnosing plants and reading seed packets; Files
and the system share sheet for the CSV, SQLite and JSON exports; haptics on the
planting grid; on-device encrypted storage.

THE OPTIONAL AI ASSISTANT ("Ask" tab)
This runs on the user's own Google Gemini or Anthropic API key, which the user
supplies in Settings. It is genuinely optional and every other feature works
without it. So that you can test it, a temporary API key with a spend cap is
below; please let us know if it has expired and we will issue another.

  Gemini API key:  <PASTE A THROWAWAY KEY WITH A SPEND CAP BEFORE SUBMITTING>

Answers generated by the assistant carry a "Report this answer" control on
every message, which captures the report on the device and offers to email it.

LOCATION
Optional. The user can type a ZIP code instead. If granted, coordinates are
used once to fetch frost dates and weather and are stored only in the encrypted
store on the device.

ENCRYPTION
Standard AES-256-GCM and PBKDF2 via Apple's own CryptoKit-backed WebCrypto, used
only to protect the user's data at rest. No proprietary cryptography.
ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption is set to false in Info.plist accordingly.
```

## Play Console — "App access" declaration

Play asks whether any part of the app is behind a login. Answer:

> **All functionality is available without special access.**

and add the note:

```
No login of any kind. The AI assistant needs a user-supplied API key from
Google, which is free to obtain, but it is optional and no other feature
depends on it.
```
